Should You Leave the Heat On for Your Dog When You Go Out?

Rather than switching the heat off completely when you go out, it's usually safer to leave it on away mode or a low temperature. Dogs are commonly said to feel comfortable at an indoor temperature of about 18–22°C (64–72°F), and puppies, senior dogs, small dogs and short-coated dogs feel the cold more. On the other hand, a floor that's too hot can cause low-temperature burns and dehydration, so the key is to give your dog both a warm spot and a cool spot.

01When you can turn it off, and when to leave it on

SituationRecommendation
Well-insulated apartment, healthy adult dog, out for 2–3 hoursAway mode or a very low setting may be enough
Midwinter with temperatures below freezing, out all dayLeave it on away mode or around 18°C (64°F)
Puppy, senior, small, short-coated or thin dogA bit warmer, around 20°C (68°F)
Dogs with a thick double coat, like a JindoA little cool is fine. Just don't let it get too hot

With the heat off, a poorly insulated home can get quite chilly within a few hours. The problem is that nobody is home to notice if your dog is cold. Away mode often runs only low enough to keep pipes from freezing, so if you'll be gone a long time in midwinter, double-check the temperature setting.

02Dogs that feel the cold more

  • Puppies: They're still not good at regulating body temperature.
  • Senior dogs: With less muscle and fat, they get cold easily. Dogs with sore joints stiffen up more in the cold.
  • Small or thin dogs: The smaller the body, the faster it loses heat.
  • Short or single coats: Dogs with little undercoat, like Chihuahuas, Dachshunds and Poodles, tend to handle cold poorly.
  • Dogs who are unwell: If your dog has heart or kidney disease or is recovering from something, ask your vet what temperature is right.

If your dog curls up tight and shivers, only burrows under blankets, or has cold ears and paws, they're cold.

03Too hot is a problem too

Homes with underfloor heating, the standard in Korea, can get floors hotter than you'd expect. A dog lying on a hot floor for a long time can get low-temperature burns on the belly and around the joints, and the dry indoor air makes them look for water more.

  • Don't set the heat too high. If the floor feels hot to your hand, it's hot for your dog too.
  • Put down a cushion or mat so there's a layer between the floor and your dog's body.
  • Leave doors open so your dog can also get to cool tile or the entryway.
  • Set out water in at least two places, filled generously.
  • If you use a humidifier, put it where your dog can't knock it over.

04Beyond the thermostat

  • A warm bed: Put a cushion with a blanket a little away from the wall. Windows and the front door let in cold drafts.
  • Turn off heating pads and space heaters before you leave: Your dog could chew the cord or get burned lying too close, and there's a fire risk.
  • Smart thermostat or home camera: Being able to check the indoor temperature and turn on the heat while you're out is a big relief.
  • Walk during the warm hours: In midwinter, keep morning and evening walks short and go out during the day. The walk safety calculator shows how risky it feels hour by hour.
Good to know

This page is general reference information, not a diagnosis. If your dog keeps shivering, seems low on energy or won't eat, talk to your vet.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is it okay to turn the heat off when I go out?

For a healthy adult dog alone for a few hours in a well-insulated home, it can be fine.
But in midwinter, or for a puppy, senior, small or short-coated dog, it's better to leave it on away mode or a low temperature.

What indoor temperature is right for a dog?

Dogs are commonly said to feel comfortable at about 18–22°C (64–72°F).
Dogs who feel the cold can use it a bit warmer, while dogs with a thick double coat, like a Jindo, are fine a little cooler.

Is it bad to turn the heat up too high?

Yes. Underfloor heating makes the floor hot, so lying on it for a long time can cause low-temperature burns, and the air gets dry.
Make sure your dog can move to a cooler spot.

Can I leave a heating pad or space heater on when I go out?

Not while your dog is alone.
They could chew the cord or get burned lying too close, and there's a fire risk.
