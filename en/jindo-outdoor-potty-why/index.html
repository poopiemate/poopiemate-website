Why do Jindos insist on pottying outside?

Jindos and Jindo mixes often hold it all day at home and only go once they're out on a walk. The usual explanation is a mix of three traits: they keep their space unusually clean, they don't change a rule once it's set, and they check their territory by scent. It's personality, not a problem to fix. What matters is making sure they get enough walks so they don't hold it too long.

015 reasons Jindos insist on going outside

ReasonWhat it means
Very clean by natureJindos groom themselves and are known for not soiling where they sleep. If they see the whole home as their space, they won't go anywhere inside.
Rules stickSmart and independent, once a Jindo learns "potty is outside" it may ignore a pad completely. This is the famous Jindo stubbornness.
Territory by scentA watchful, territorial breed. Sniffing other dogs' marks on a walk and leaving their own is an important daily job.
Walking gets things movingBred to help hunters on Jindo Island, they're built to walk a lot. Walking and sniffing get the gut moving, so going mid-walk feels natural.
Good at holding itAs a medium-sized dog, a Jindo can hold it longer than a small dog, so it chooses to wait rather than go inside. That's exactly the part to watch.

02Is a Jindo mix the same?

Mixes with strong Jindo traits often insist on going outside too. Some rescues and fosters learned to go on dirt or grass before adoption.

On the other hand, Jindo mixes raised on pads from puppyhood often use them fine. The first habit a dog learns matters more than the breed. Training steps are in Jindo mix potty training.

03Is it okay that they insist?

Going outside isn't the problem. Holding it too long is. Rain, storms, cold snaps or a late night at work can mean a long wait, and repeated long waits strain the bladder and bowels.

  • 3–4 times a day: the usual number of potty chances for an adult. Morning, midday, evening and before bed.
  • No gaps over 8 hours: avoid days where there's no chance for more than 8 hours.
  • Short trips on bad days: stepping outside just to go is enough.

What can go wrong when they hold it is covered in Why outdoor-only dogs shouldn't hold it.

Good to know

This is general information, not a diagnosis. If your dog hasn't peed for over a day or strains without anything coming out, see a vet right away.

04If you'd like a backup indoor spot

Pushing a Jindo makes it hold it even longer. Think of it as adding an option, not replacing the outdoors.

  • Start near the door: put artificial grass by the entrance or on a balcony rather than mid-living room.
  • Bring the walk home: place a tissue with a bit of pee from the walk on the mat.
  • Go at the right moments: right after waking up or a little after meals.
  • Praise big: treat during or right after. Clean up accidents quietly, no scolding.

Many Jindos still won't use it after months. Then more walks is the realistic answer. Why dogs prefer outside in general: Why do dogs only potty outside?

05Outdoor-only, but living indoors

Going outside to potty doesn't mean living outside. Your Jindo can live indoors with the family and simply get plenty of walks. When the family splits 3–4 walks a day, though, "did she poop?" becomes the most asked question at home.

Why won't my Jindo potty inside?

Jindos are known for keeping their living space very clean, and once they learn "potty happens outside" they rarely change the rule.
Checking territory by scent plays a part too.

Can a Jindo learn to use a pad indoors?

Some do, but many never use one.
Try artificial grass by the door or on a balcony with a scent from the walk; if it doesn't take, more walks is the realistic answer.

Do Jindo mixes insist on going outside too?

Mixes with strong Jindo traits often do.
Some rescues also learned to go on dirt or grass before adoption.

How many potty walks does an outdoor-only Jindo need?

Adults are commonly given 3–4 potty chances a day.
At minimum go out first thing and before bed, and avoid days with more than 8 hours between chances.
