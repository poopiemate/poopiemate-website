How Cold Is Too Cold to Walk Your Dog?A Feels-Like Temperature Chart by Size

For winter walks, go by the feels-like temperature, not the thermometer. At 3°C (37°F) with wind, it can feel below freezing. By the POOPIEMATE walk safety index, small dogs need care once it feels below 10°C (50°F), and around a feels-like 0°C (32°F) even an average dog should take a short potty-first walk. A double-coated dog used to the cold, like a Jindo, can walk as usual at -5°C (23°F). Here is how cold is too cold for your dog, by size, coat and age.

Smaller dogs have more skin surface for their weight, so they lose heat faster and feel the cold first. Large dogs hold heat longer, which buys them about one level.

02Adjust for your own dog

When it feels 10°C (50°F) or colder, add or subtract these from the chart level. Halves round up.

FactorLevel
Small dog+1
Short coat (Chihuahua, Dachshund, French Bulldog, a Poodle or Bichon clipped short)+1
Rain or snow likely (40% or more)+1
Puppy under 6 months or senior dog+0.5
Double or heavy coat (Jindo, Shiba, Husky, Samoyed)−1
Used to the cold (walks outside every day all winter)−1

Wet fur pulls heat away much faster than dry fur, so a rainy or snowy day counts one level higher at the same temperature.

At the same -5°C, the Maltese is at level 5 and the Jindo mix at level 2. That is why "how cold is too cold" has no single answer. To try your own dog, open the walk safety calculator and it will use the feels-like temperature where you are right now.

04What to do at each level

  • Levels 1–2: Walk as usual. At level 2, just watch for shivering.
  • Level 3: Keep it to 15–20 minutes. Put a coat on small or short-coated dogs.
  • Level 4: Coat on, potty first, keep it short. Protect the paws and wipe them as soon as you get home.
  • Level 5: Potty only, five minutes or less. Do play and exercise indoors.

If your dog only goes outside

Even at level 5, a dog who only potties outdoors still has to go out, and holding it for too long isn't good either (what happens when dogs hold it too long). On days like this, split the walk into several quick potty trips. Head straight to the usual spot near home and come back in once they're done.

05How to make winter walks less cold

  • Move walks into daylight: The warmest part of a winter day is usually early afternoon. Early mornings and after dark feel much colder.
  • Avoid the wind: Riversides, bridges and gaps between tall buildings can feel several degrees colder. Sheltered side streets are better in winter.
  • Pick a coat that covers the belly: The belly has the thinnest fur. See our winter food and coat guide.
  • Short and often: Three 10-minute walks chill the body less than one 30-minute walk.
  • Dry them right away: After snow play, towel off and use a lukewarm hair dryer on the belly and between the toes.

Paws and de-icing salt

De-icing salt can crack paw pads and upset the stomach if licked off. After the walk, rinse paws with lukewarm water and dry between the toes. Use a paw balm on any cracks. Booties are great if your dog is used to them.

06Signs your dog is too cold

  • Shivering or walking hunched up
  • Lifting paws in turn, or stopping and refusing to walk
  • Turning back toward home again and again
  • Cold ears and paws

If you see these, head home. If the shivering doesn't stop indoors, or your dog turns limp with pale gums, their body temperature has dropped a lot. Wrap them in a blanket and call your vet right away.

Good to know

The levels in this guide follow the POOPIEMATE walk safety index and are not a diagnosis. If your dog has a heart or breathing condition or struggles with cold, ask your vet how long to walk.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How cold is too cold to walk a dog?

Go by the feels-like temperature.
By the POOPIEMATE walk safety index, small dogs need care below a feels-like 10°C (50°F), and around 0°C (32°F) even an average dog should take a short potty-first walk.
A double-coated dog used to the cold can walk as usual at -5°C (23°F).

Can I walk my dog below freezing?

A healthy double-coated adult is often fine down to about -5°C (23°F).
Small, short-coated or senior dogs should wear a coat and keep it to a 5–10 minute potty walk.
Below a feels-like -10°C (14°F) the base level is 5, so even a cold-hardy double-coated dog should keep it short.

Should small dogs skip walks in winter?

Rather than skipping, go out briefly and more often, in daylight, with a coat.
Walks matter for potty and stress relief, so not going out at all can cause other problems.

Is it OK to walk my dog in the snow?

Snow soaks the fur and drains body heat faster, so count it one level higher.
Dry your dog off right after, and always wash the paws if the path was salted.

How long should winter walks be?

At levels 1–2 walk as usual, at level 3 keep it to 15–20 minutes, at level 4 go short and potty first, and at level 5 potty only within five minutes.
Make up for the rest with sniffing games or tug indoors.
