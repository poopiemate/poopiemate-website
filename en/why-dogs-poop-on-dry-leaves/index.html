Why Do Dogs Like to Poop on Dry Leaves?

If your dog always stops at a pile of dry leaves on fall walks, that's perfectly normal. The usual explanation: leaf piles hold other dogs' scents for a long time, they're soft enough to squat on comfortably, and less poop ends up on paws. A habit of seeking out the feel of the ground they first pooped on as a puppy plays a part too.

014 reasons dogs love pooping on leaves

ReasonWhy it matters
Lots of scent informationRain and wind don't wash scent off a leaf pile quickly, so traces left by other dogs linger. It's where your dog catches up on the neighborhood news and leaves a message of their own.
Soft and comfortableSquatting is easier than on hard pavement, and less poop gets on paws or the fur around the rear.
A surface they learned onDogs are known to keep preferring the feel of the ground where they first learned to go (grass, dirt, leaves and so on).
Good coverDogs are vulnerable while pooping, so many pick a spot with a little cover, like bushes or a leaf pile.

Dogs who only go outdoors tend to have especially clear surface preferences. For why some dogs only poop outside, see why some dogs only potty outdoors.

02Why dogs circle before they poop

Circling is commonly explained as choosing a spot, pressing the leaves flat and checking that nothing dangerous is nearby. A 2013 study that observed 70 dogs made headlines by finding that dogs line their bodies up north–south when Earth's magnetic field is calm, but not every dog does this, so take it as a fun fact.

Kicking leaves with the back legs is common too. The reasons are in why dogs kick their back legs after pooping.

03What to watch out for in leaf piles

  • Ticks: Common in fall leaves and brush. After a walk, check inside the ears, the armpits and between the toes.
  • Chestnut burrs, thorns, broken glass: Hard to see under leaves. If your dog licks a paw or limps, take a look.
  • Mushrooms, ginkgo nuts, rotting food: Keep the leash short so your dog doesn't eat them.
  • Other animals' droppings: Some dogs sniff and then eat them or roll in them.
  • Wet leaves: Slippery and prone to mold.

04How to pick up poop on leaves cleanly

  • Double up two bags over your hand and grab it leaves and all. The leaves wrap around the poop, so it's actually cleaner.
  • A grabber or a portable scoop makes it even easier.
  • Poop can blend in with leaf colors, so take a look at the color and firmness before you pick it up. You can check it against the dog poop condition guide.
Good to know

This page is general reference information, not a diagnosis. If your dog limps after a walk, or you see ticks or wounds on the skin, talk to your vet.
