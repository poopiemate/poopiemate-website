Which Korean brands don't test on beagles?

Most dogs used in labs are beagles, because they're gentle and trust people. Living with a dog, news like this doesn't feel distant anymore. So we started with what we use every day, detergent and skincare, and looked for Korean brands that don't test on animals.

01The news that started this

  • 77 lab beagles: Korean public institutions bought 77 beagles for experiments through the government procurement system. (Jodal Economy, Oct 6, 2026, Korean)
  • 4.95 million lab animals: Korea used 4,953,746 animals in experiments in 2025, more than the year before, and 52.7% were in the most painful category. (DailyVet, Oct 7, 2026, Korean)
  • After the experiment: a column on how little is known about what happens to animals once experiments end. (Segye Ilbo, Nov 12, 2026, Korean)

02Cruelty-free vs. vegan

TermMeaning
Cruelty-freeNot tested on animals, including ingredients and ingredient suppliers.
VeganNo animal-derived ingredients. Says nothing about testing by itself.

03Certification or self-claim: what to trust

EvidenceWho checks it
Leaping BunnyThe strictest. Supply-chain monitoring and independent audits.
PETAThe company and its suppliers sign a no-animal-testing statement.
Vegan certificationChecks each product for animal ingredients and testing. Only certified products count.
Brand's own claimThe brand says it doesn't test. Not necessarily false, but nobody verified it.

Trust them roughly in that order. Vague words like "vegan formula" or "naturally derived" alone aren't evidence. Look for the certifier's name, check whether it covers the whole brand or a few products, and search PETA's cruelty-free database yourself.

06If you live with a dog

Detergent, softener and deodorizer end up on the floors and blankets your dog sniffs and licks every day. Choosing products that aren't tested on animals, with gentle ingredients, is good for the dog at home too. We hope more lab beagles get to leave and find a family.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

If a product is vegan, was it not tested on animals?

Not necessarily.
Vegan means no animal-derived ingredients; animal testing is a separate question.
Some vegan certifications, like the Korea Agency of Vegan Certification, do also require no animal testing.

Can I trust PETA's cruelty-free list?

Companies and their ingredient suppliers sign a statement that they don't test on animals.
It's less strict than Leaping Bunny, which adds supply-chain monitoring and audits, but stronger than a brand's own claim.

Doesn't Korea ban animal testing for cosmetics?

Since 2017 Korea's Cosmetics Act bans selling cosmetics tested on animals, with exceptions such as when an export country requires it.
Household goods like detergent, toothpaste and pads are not cosmetics, so the ban doesn't cover them.

A brand isn't on PETA's list. Does it test on animals?

Not necessarily.
Since August 2025 PETA only accepts new companies from the US, Canada, Germany and India, so newer Korean household brands can't easily join.
Check other certifications or the brand's official policy.
